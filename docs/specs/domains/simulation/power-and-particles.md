# Power, particles, lasers and logic

## Purpose
Defines BlokDust's "physics": what powers a sound source, how particles and laser beams travel and collide, and how logic blocks react, all in deterministic engine time.

## Requirements
**Power**
- **R1.** A source is *powered* while its power count > 0 (v1 `AddPower`/`RemovePower` counting). A powered audio source sounds (gate on); unpowered releases (gate off).
- **R2.** `Power` powers every connected source while connected. `Toggle` powers connected sources only while its `on` state is true. `Pulse` powers connected sources for `pulseLength` seconds (default 0.15 s, v1 `Config.PulseLength`) each time it is triggered.
- **R3.** Emitters and Lasers run when powered or when `selfPowered` is true.

**Particles**
- **R4.** A running Emitter emits one particle every `interval` seconds (v1 default `rate` 80 frames → 1.333 s at the 60 fps reference), at `angle` degrees (−90 = up), with `speed` (v1 default 3 units/frame → 180 units/s) and `range` (v1 default 600 units). Lifetime = range / speed. Counter semantics follow v1: a newly created emitter starts its counter at 0, so a self-powered emitter first fires after one interval; while unpowered the counter is primed, so an emitter fires immediately when it becomes powered.
- **R4a.** *(v2, CHG-0014)* `offset` (0–1, default 0 = v1 timing) nudges an emitter by that fraction of its interval. Synced: its whole beat grid is late by `offset × division`. Unsynced: the primed counter is short by `offset × interval`, so the first particle after power arrives comes that much later; the ones after it are an interval apart as before. Tested in `test_compressor.cpp`.
- **R5.** Each tick, a particle collides with the topmost block (highest z) whose hit outline contains its position, excluding Emitters (but see R5b). Effects: `Void` absorbs it; `Toggle`/`Pulse` perform their logic; a source receives a **pulse** of power for `pulseLength` (0.15 s). The particle is consumed on any collision.
- **R5b.** *(v2, owner 2026-11-11, SIMULATION-0004; v1 let particles pass through emitters)* A particle that enters another Emitter (inside its hit outline now, outside it the tick before) is consumed (event `Hit` on the emitter), and that emitter fires one particle at once, whether or not it is powered. An emitter never catches particles born inside it: its own, or those of an emitter overlapping it. Tested in `test_emitter_hit.cpp`.
- **R5c.** *(v2, CHG-0015, SIMULATION-0005)* `Counter` and `Chance` are **gates**: never powered, never connected. A particle that enters one (as R5b: inside now, outside the tick before, and not born there) is decided on once: it carries on unchanged (event `Pass`; nothing under the gate sees it that tick) or is absorbed (event `Block`). Counter: each hit takes the next step of the Euclidean pattern E(`hits`, `steps`) read from `rotate` (step i passes iff ((i + rotate) mod steps) × hits mod steps < hits), then the count moves on; it restarts when the world powers on and at a transport start. Chance: passes with probability `chance`, from the block's own random stream (seeded by block id, so the patch seed stays visual-only, R12). Tested in `test_gates.cpp`.
- **R5d.** *(v2, CHG-0015, SIMULATION-0006)* A particle that enters a `Prism` is consumed (event `Split`) and, after the particle pass, leaves from the Prism's centre as `streams` (1–4) particles fanned evenly across `spread` degrees around `angle`, each at the particle's speed and with the life it had left. They are born inside the Prism, so it never catches them. `rotate` turns `angle` as a Laser's (R9), whenever the world is on. When the particle pool is full the rest are dropped.
- **R6.** Particle size is 2 + rand(0..1) units from the patch PRNG (visual only).

**Lasers**
- **R7.** A running Laser casts a beam from its position at `angle` for `range` units (v1 default 275). The beam MUST stop at the nearest `Void` it intersects. Every source/logic block whose outline intersects the beam before that point is *hit*.
- **R7a.** *(v2, CHG-0015)* Along a beam, the nearest of these stops it: a `Void`; a `Counter` or `Chance` closed to it; a `Prism`. A gate decides when a laser's beam arrives (event `Pass`/`Block`, `other` = the laser) and keeps that answer for as long as that laser keeps reaching it. At a Prism the beam carries on from the Prism's centre as its streams (R5d's angles), each with the length the beam had left. A path passes through at most 8 Prisms and never the same one twice (it goes straight through instead); one laser casts at most 64 beams. A laser is never hit by its own beams.
- **R7b.** *(v2, CHG-0016, SIMULATION-0007)* A `Sound Laser` casts a beam as a Laser does (R7, R7a; `angle`, `range`, `rotate` while on), but what turns it on is sound: the level of the sources connected to it. It hears each of their chains as it sounds (after the chain's effects, mono), through `band` (0 everything; 1 below 250 Hz; 2 between 250 Hz and 2.5 kHz; 3 above 2.5 kHz), as a peak that falls with a 50 ms time constant. Each tick the beam is on if that level is above `threshold` dB (`invert`: below it), and stays on for at least `hold` seconds after. The level is the one at the tick's sample, so the beam follows the sound within a tick. It is not a source: nothing powers it and particles pass through it. Up to 8 listen at once (`kMaxListeners`); more stay dark. Tested in `test_sound_laser.cpp` (golden `sound-laser`).
- **R8.** A newly hit source gains power while hit (continuous, not a pulse); a newly hit logic block performs its logic once; when a source stops being hit it loses that power.
- **R9.** With `rotate` ≠ 0 and running, the angle advances by `rotate` deg/s (v1: rotate/100 deg per frame → ×0.6 deg/s at 60 fps).
- **R10.** Lasers can hit and power other Emitters and Lasers (power sources are sources in v1).

**Time and determinism**
- **R10b.** A source's ADSR at gate-on: the default (A 0.01, D 0.5, S 0.5, R 0.01 s) when the gate is held by static power (Power, Toggle); otherwise the settings of the last Envelope block in its chain. This reproduces v1, where pre-effects apply after Power triggers (docs/reference/v1-audio.md). Tested in `test_audio_graph.cpp`.
- **R10c.** Holding the pointer on a source (audio source, Particle Emitter, Laser: v1 `Source` subclasses) powers it until release (v1 `Source.MouseDown/MouseUp` → `AddPower/RemovePower`), including a block just dragged out of a menu; engine command `Press`. Pressing effects, Power, Toggle or Pulse does nothing. Like a particle hit, a press uses the chain's Envelope (R10b).
- **R10d.** Sources have voices as v1 `Source` (`GetVoice`/`RemoveVoice`): voice 0 is shared by power and mono controllers (the mono voice); a connected controller with Poly on gives the source 9 voices, each key its own (none free: the oldest is stolen without retrigger). A mono key pressed while the mono voice sounds changes pitch without retriggering, gliding linearly over the controller's `glide` seconds (only when ≤ 1 voice is active); releasing the sounding key releases the voice (an older held key does not take over); if the source is powered, power takes the voice back at the default note. Disconnecting a controller releases its voices.
- **R10e.** *(v2, CHG-0017, AUDIO-0002)* A source is **arranged** while a note effect (Chord, Arpeggiator, Random Note) is in its chain or a connected keyboard has `keysSetPitch` on; otherwise R10d applies untouched. Changing over starts clean (voices released, held keys forgotten). An arranged source has up to 9 voices and takes its notes like this. *Notes in:* the keys held on its keyboards, or, with none held, its own note. A sounding mono keyboard counts only its newest held key; a keyboard with Keys set pitch counts every held key and never sounds by itself. *Gate:* the notes sound while the source has power (R1) or a sounding key is held; a *trigger* is power arriving or a sounding key being pressed. Notes that stay in the set keep sounding; ones that leave are released; new ones start (their envelope as R10b: the default under a Power block alone, else the chain's). Glide does not apply. Per-note expression still follows each note's key and channel.
- **R10f.** *(CHG-0017)* `Chord` adds its intervals to every note in (octave, fifth, major, minor, sus2, sus4, seventh, major 7th, minor 7th); `inversion` raises that many of its lowest notes an octave.
- **R10g.** *(CHG-0017)* `Arpeggiator` sounds one of the notes at a time, struck afresh at each step, in pitch order repeated up through `octaves`: `pattern` up, down, up-and-down or random (its own seeded stream). With `perHit` each trigger moves to the next note. Otherwise it starts on its first note when the source starts sounding and steps by itself: every `division` beats when `sync` is on, `speed` times a second when not; keys and hits meanwhile change the notes, not where it is.
- **R10h.** *(CHG-0017)* `Random Note` replaces the notes in with one picked at each trigger: among the held keys if there are any, else from `scale` (major, minor, dorian, lydian, mixolydian, pentatonic) on `key`, octaves `low`…`high` (0 = the octave from middle C), from its own seeded stream (the patch seed stays visual-only). Order when several note effects share a chain: Random Note, then Chord, then Arpeggiator. Tested in `test_note_effects.cpp` (goldens `keys-strum`, `arp-hits`, `random-note`).
- **R13.** *(v2, CHG-0021, SCOPE-0006)* A `Stage` is an audio source whose sound is an **inner world**: a second set of blocks, connections, particles and beams that nothing outside can touch. Its power is that world's power switch: unpowered, the inner world is off (silent, as the world POWER switch off); powered (R1: Power, Toggle, Pulse, a beam, a particle's pulse, a held pointer) it runs. The inner world steps on the outer world's ticks and tempo (aligned when it is attached), has no master stage of its own, and its stereo mix × the Stage's `gain` enters the Stage's chain like any source's sound, so outer effects apply. A Stage inside an inner world has no world (depth 2). At most `kMaxStages` (8) Stages hold a world; there is no other limit. Keyboards and note effects do not reach into a Stage. Samplers and the microphone inside a Stage have no audio yet.
- **R11.** All of the above run on the engine's fixed 240 Hz tick, derived from the sample clock; trigger events carry sample offsets.
- **R12.** Same patch + seed + inputs ⇒ bit-identical event stream and audio on every platform.

## Scenarios
- **S1.** Self-powered Emitter aimed right at a Tone 10 gu away: Tone receives a 0.15 s gate every 1.333 s, first hit at a deterministic sample index.
- **S2.** Laser through Tone A, then Void, then Tone B: A is powered, B is not (R7).
- **S3.** Particle hits a Toggle connected to a Tone: Tone toggles on; next particle toggles it off.
