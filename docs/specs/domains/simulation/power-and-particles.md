# Power, particles, lasers and logic

## Purpose
Defines BlokDust's "physics": what powers a sound source, how particles and laser beams travel and collide, and how logic blocks react, all in deterministic engine time.

## Requirements
**Power**
- **R1.** A source is *powered* while its power count > 0 (v1 `AddPower`/`RemovePower` counting). A powered audio source sounds (gate on); unpowered releases (gate off).
- **R1a.** *(CHG-0035, owner 2026-11-17; v1 `Microphone.TriggerRelease` muted it unless powered)* The website's `Microphone` is muted until it is played like any other source: it passes its input while one of its voices sounds (power, a held key, a hit's pulse) and is silent otherwise, ramping 5 ms each way. In the plugins the same block is the host's Audio Input and stays always on (PLUGIN-0002), as does `AudioIn`.
- **R1b.** *(CHG-0037, owner 2026-11-18)* Trashing an emitter leaves its particles flying (v1), but CLEAR and opening a patch start the world over: the engine's whole-world `Reset` (id 0) removes every particle in flight, in its Stages too.
- **R2.** `Power` powers every connected source while connected. `Toggle` powers connected sources only while its `on` state is true. `Pulse` powers connected sources for `pulseLength` seconds (default 0.15 s, v1 `Config.PulseLength`) each time it is triggered.
- **R2a.** *(v2, SIMULATION-0009)* `Power` with `gapOnHit` on: a particle that hits it is consumed and a beam that arrives at it (once per arrival) cuts its power for `gap` s (≥ 1 tick; a new hit restarts it), so its sources let go and are struck again when it returns. Off (default), particles and beams pass through it as in v1.
- **R2b.** *(v2, CHG-0029, SIMULATION-0010)* A `Pulse` with `delay` > 0 (synced: `delayBeats` > 0 at the tempo when hit) does not fire on the hit: it fires that long after it, at the start of the tick, powering the sources connected to it then for its length then. Each hit keeps its own wait (none restarts another), up to `kMaxPulseDelays` (16) per Pulse; more are dropped. The world POWER switch off and a transport start forget waiting hits. 0 (default) is R2. Tested in `test_pulse_delay.cpp` (golden `pulse-strum`).
- **R2c.** *(v2, CHG-0015, SIMULATION-0005)* `Counter` is a switch with steps. Each hit (R5, R8) takes the next step of the Euclidean pattern E(`hits`, `steps`) read from `rotate` (step i is active iff ((i + rotate) mod steps) × hits mod steps < hits) or, when `pattern` ≥ 0, of its steps set by hand (step i active iff bit i of `pattern` is set; moving Steps, Hits or Rotate in the panel sets `pattern` to −1). While its step is active it powers its connected blocks, as a Toggle that is on does; a hit that lands on an empty step turns it off. Consecutive active steps keep it on. Its count goes back to step 1, off, when its world powers on (the world POWER switch, a transport start, its Stage gaining power, R13) and on the panel's Reset (engine command `ResetCounter`).
- **R2d.** *(v2, CHG-0015, SIMULATION-0005)* `Chance` is a switch set at random. Each hit (R5, R8) rolls once: with probability `chance` (event `Pass`) it is on and powers its connected blocks, as a Toggle that is on does; otherwise (event `Block`) it is off. It stays as the last roll left it until the next hit, and starts off when its world powers on. The rolls come from the block's own random stream (seeded by block id, so the patch seed stays visual-only, R12). Tested in `test_chance.cpp` (golden `chance`).
- **R3.** Emitters and Lasers run when powered or when `selfPowered` is true.

**Particles**
- **R4.** A running Emitter emits one particle every `interval` seconds (v1 default `rate` 80 frames → 1.333 s at the 60 fps reference), at `angle` degrees (−90 = up), with `speed` (v1 default 3 units/frame → 180 units/s) and `range` (v1 default 600 units). Lifetime = range / speed. Counter semantics follow v1: a newly created emitter starts its counter at 0, so a self-powered emitter first fires after one interval; while unpowered the counter is primed, so an emitter fires immediately when it becomes powered.
- **R4a.** *(v2, CHG-0014)* `offset` (0–1, default 0 = v1 timing) nudges an emitter by that fraction of its interval. Synced: its whole beat grid is late by `offset × division`. Unsynced: the primed counter is short by `offset × interval`, so the first particle after power arrives comes that much later; the ones after it are an interval apart as before. Tested in `test_compressor.cpp`.
- **R5.** Each tick, a particle collides with the topmost block (highest z) whose hit outline contains its position, excluding Emitters (but see R5b). Effects: `Void` (and a closed `Gate`, R5c) absorbs it; `Toggle`/`Pulse`/`Counter`/`Chance` perform their logic; a source receives a **pulse** of power for `pulseLength` (0.15 s). The particle is consumed on any collision.
- **R5e.** *(v2, SIMULATION-0008, SIMULATION-0012)* A hit (or a Pulse or Counter firing) on a source that only earlier hits' pulses are holding strikes it again on that tick (envelope from the start; an arranged source re-triggers its notes, so Random Note picks again). Power ends when the last pulse ends, as before.
- **R5b.** *(v2, owner 2026-11-11, SIMULATION-0004; v1 let particles pass through emitters)* A particle that enters another Emitter (inside its hit outline now, outside it the tick before) is consumed (event `Hit` on the emitter), and that emitter fires one particle at once, whether or not it is powered. An emitter never catches particles born inside it: its own, or those of an emitter overlapping it. Tested in `test_emitter_hit.cpp`.
- **R5c.** *(v2, CHG-0032, SIMULATION-0011)* A `Gate` is open while a power block connected to it (Power, Toggle, Pulse, Counter, Chance) powers it, and closed otherwise; `invert` swaps the two. Closed, it absorbs particles as a `Void` does (event `Absorb`); open, they pass as if it were not there. Particles and beams that reach it never switch it. Its state is read every tick. Tested in `test_gate.cpp` (golden `gate`).
- **R5d.** *(v2, CHG-0015, SIMULATION-0006)* A particle that enters a `Prism` is consumed (event `Split`) and, after the particle pass, leaves from the Prism's centre as `streams` (1–4) particles fanned evenly across `spread` degrees around `angle`, each at the particle's speed and with the life it had left. They are born inside the Prism, so it never catches them. `rotate` turns `angle` as a Laser's (R9), whenever the world is on. When the particle pool is full the rest are dropped.
- **R6.** Particle size is 2 + rand(0..1) units from the patch PRNG (visual only).

**Lasers**
- **R7.** A running Laser casts a beam from its position at `angle` for `range` units (v1 default 275). The beam MUST stop at the nearest `Void` it intersects. Every source/logic block whose outline intersects the beam before that point is *hit*.
- **R7a.** *(v2, CHG-0015)* Along a beam, the nearest of these stops it: a `Void`; a closed `Gate` (R7c); a `Prism`. At a Prism the beam carries on from the Prism's centre as its streams (R5d's angles), each with the length the beam had left. A path passes through at most 8 Prisms and never the same one twice (it goes straight through instead); one laser casts at most 64 beams. A laser is never hit by its own beams.
- **R7b.** *(v2, CHG-0016, SIMULATION-0007)* A `Sound Laser` casts a beam as a Laser does (R7, R7a; `angle`, `range`; it does not turn), but what turns it on is sound: the level of the sources connected to it. It hears each of their chains as it sounds (after the chain's effects, mono), through `band` (0 everything; 1 below 250 Hz; 2 between 250 Hz and 2.5 kHz; 3 above 2.5 kHz), as a peak that falls with a 50 ms time constant. Each tick the beam is on if that level is above `threshold` dB (`invert`: below it), and stays on for at least `hold` seconds after. The level is the one at the tick's sample, so the beam follows the sound within a tick. It is not a source: nothing powers it and particles pass through it. Up to 8 listen at once (`kMaxListeners`); more stay dark. Tested in `test_sound_laser.cpp` (golden `sound-laser`).
- **R7c.** *(v2, CHG-0032, SIMULATION-0011)* A closed `Gate` stops a beam as a `Void` does; an open one lets it through. A beam is cut or restored on the tick the Gate closes or opens.
- **R8.** A newly hit source gains power while hit (continuous, not a pulse); a newly hit logic block performs its logic once; when a source stops being hit it loses that power.
- **R9.** With `rotate` ≠ 0 and running, the angle advances by `rotate` deg/s (v1: rotate/100 deg per frame → ×0.6 deg/s at 60 fps).
- **R9a.** *(v2, CHG-0034)* With `restart` on, a Laser starts again from its `angle` each time it starts turning: a free one when it gains power (Toggle, Pulse, Counter step, Power, a beam or a press), a synced one when it starts running, its turn then counted from that beat instead of beat 0. A transport start (SIMULATION-0003) counts as a start. Off (default) is v1: it carries on from where it stopped.
- **R10.** Lasers can hit and power other Emitters and Lasers (power sources are sources in v1).
- **R10a.** *(v2, SIMULATION-0012)* A pulse (a particle hit, a Pulse firing, a Counter's active step) that reaches an Emitter or Laser only hits hold (no Power, Toggle, press, key or `selfPoweredMode`) fires it again on its next tick: an Emitter fires one particle and starts its timing over; a Laser's beam hits again every block it is on (R8's newly-hit actions; a source not held by Power, Toggle or press is struck again as R5e; an Emitter or Laser only hits hold fires again). A Pulse or Counter reaching a source strikes it again as R5e. Power is unchanged. Tested in `test_lasers.cpp` (golden `laser-strike`).

**Time and determinism**
- **R10b.** A source's ADSR at gate-on: the default (A 0.01, D 0.5, S 0.5, R 0.01 s) when the gate is held by static power (Power, Toggle); otherwise the settings of the last Envelope block in its chain. This reproduces v1, where pre-effects apply after Power triggers (docs/reference/v1-audio.md). Tested in `test_audio_graph.cpp`.
- **R10c.** Holding the pointer on a source (audio source, Particle Emitter, Laser: v1 `Source` subclasses) powers it until release (v1 `Source.MouseDown/MouseUp` → `AddPower/RemovePower`), including a block just dragged out of a menu; engine command `Press`. Pressing effects, Power, Toggle or Pulse does nothing. Like a particle hit, a press uses the chain's Envelope (R10b).
- **R10d.** Sources have voices as v1 `Source` (`GetVoice`/`RemoveVoice`): voice 0 is shared by power and mono controllers (the mono voice); a connected controller with Poly on gives the source 9 voices, each key its own (none free: the oldest is stolen without retrigger). A mono key pressed while the mono voice sounds changes pitch without retriggering, gliding linearly over the controller's `glide` seconds (only when ≤ 1 voice is active) — except a Granular, whose next grains take the new note at once and whose sounding grains keep theirs (v1 `Granular.SetPitch`; owner bug 2026-11-14: its pitch never left the first note, `test_granular.cpp`); releasing the sounding key releases the voice (an older held key does not take over); if the source is powered, power takes the voice back at the default note. Disconnecting a controller releases its voices.
- **R10e.** *(v2, CHG-0017, AUDIO-0002)* A source is **arranged** while a note effect (Chord, Arpeggiator, Random Note) is in its chain or a connected keyboard has `keysSetPitch` on; otherwise R10d applies untouched. Changing over starts clean (voices released, held keys forgotten). An arranged source has up to 9 voices and takes its notes like this. *Notes in:* the keys held on its keyboards, or, with none held, its own note. A sounding mono keyboard counts only its newest held key; a keyboard with Keys set pitch counts every held key and never sounds by itself. *Gate:* the notes sound while the source has power (R1) or a sounding key is held; a *trigger* is power arriving or a sounding key being pressed. Notes that stay in the set keep sounding; ones that leave are released; new ones start (their envelope as R10b: the default under a Power block alone, else the chain's). Glide does not apply. Per-note expression still follows each note's key and channel.
- **R10f.** *(CHG-0017, CHG-0040)* `Chord` has a quality for each of the 12 notes (`q0`…`q11`, C…B), bottom to top: None, Sus2, Minor, Major, Sus4, Minor 7th, 7th, Major 7th; defaults C Maj, C♯ Maj7, D m7, E♭ Maj, E m, F Maj7, F♯ Maj7, G 7, A♭ Maj7, A m7, B♭ Maj, B m. `key` (0…11) shifts the pattern: a note in with pitch class `n` plays the quality stored at `(n − key) mod 12` above it (None: the note alone). `smooth` (default on): the note in stays put and each other chord note goes to the octave closest to the previous chord's notes, within an octave of the note in; the first chord after the source changes over (R10e) and every chord with `smooth` off are in root position. With several notes in, a new chord leads from the newest chord.
- **R10g.** *(CHG-0017)* `Arpeggiator` sounds one of the notes at a time, struck afresh at each step, in pitch order repeated up through `octaves`: `pattern` up, down, up-and-down or random (its own seeded stream). With `perHit` each trigger moves to the next note. Otherwise it starts on its first note when the source starts sounding and steps by itself: every `division` beats when `sync` is on, `speed` times a second when not; keys and hits meanwhile change the notes, not where it is.
- **R10h.** *(CHG-0017, CHG-0040)* `Random Note` has a weight for each of the 12 notes (`w0`…`w11`, 0…1; default C major: white notes 1, black 0), shifted by `key` as R10f, and a range `low`…`high` in MIDI notes (24…107, default 60…71, `low` ≤ `high`). Notes in only trigger: each note in gets its own note, drawn from every note in the range weighted by its pitch class's weight, from the block's own seeded stream (the patch seed stays visual-only), when the note starts and again at each trigger (R5e); its velocity and per-note expression are kept, its pitch is not. With all weights 0, or no weighted note in the range, the note in plays unchanged. Order when several note effects share a chain: Random Note, then Chord, then Arpeggiator. Tested in `test_note_effects.cpp` (goldens `keys-strum`, `arp-hits`, `random-note`).
- **R13.** *(v2, CHG-0021, SCOPE-0006)* A `Stage` is an audio source whose sound is an **inner world**: a second set of blocks, connections, particles and beams that nothing outside can touch. Its power is that world's power switch: unpowered, the inner world is off (silent, as the world POWER switch off); powered (R1: Power, Toggle, Pulse, a beam, a particle's pulse, a held pointer) it runs. The inner world steps on the outer world's ticks and tempo (aligned when it is attached), has no master stage of its own, and its stereo mix × the Stage's `gain` enters the Stage's chain like any source's sound, so outer effects apply. A Stage inside an inner world has no world (depth 2). At most `kMaxStages` (8) Stages hold a world; there is no other limit. Note effects outside do not reach into a Stage. Samplers inside a Stage play the audio the host attaches to the inner world, and the host input (Microphone, Audio In) reaches it as it reaches the outer world.
- **R13a.** *(v2, UI-0012)* Notes reach inside a Stage two ways. (1) A note sent to every controller of a type (the computer's keys, MIDI in) reaches the controllers of that type in every inner world too. (2) A note played on a controller that touches a Stage goes in to every controller of its inner world (Keyboards, MIDI Inputs, Touchpads, Stage Ins), each playing what it touches with its own settings, the key moved by the outer controller's octave; controllers of the type that heard it by (1) are skipped, so a key is heard once. Expression follows its note in. A note ends on the key it went in as, and ends when its controller stops touching the Stage. Notes play while the Stage is unpowered (as keys do in a world switched off).
- **R13b.** *(v2, owner 2026-11-14)* A Stage's `keepParticles` (Keep Particles, off by default): when on and the Stage loses its power, the particles in flight inside carry on (moving, ageing, hitting: sources sound, Emitters catch and fire per R5b, Counters, Chances and Prisms act) while the inner world is otherwise off (no emission, no Power, Toggle or beams). Off, or turned off while the Stage is unpowered, they vanish. The world POWER switch clears the particles in every world, kept or not. Tested in `test_stage.cpp`.
- **R11.** All of the above run on the engine's fixed 240 Hz tick, derived from the sample clock; trigger events carry sample offsets.
- **R12.** Same patch + seed + inputs ⇒ bit-identical event stream and audio on every platform.

## Scenarios
- **S1.** Self-powered Emitter aimed right at a Tone 10 gu away: Tone receives a 0.15 s gate every 1.333 s, first hit at a deterministic sample index.
- **S2.** Laser through Tone A, then Void, then Tone B: A is powered, B is not (R7).
- **S3.** Particle hits a Toggle connected to a Tone: Tone toggles on; next particle toggles it off.
