BlokDust allows for the creation of synths, loops, effects and self-playing environments. Blocks connect when they come close to each other. A [Tone](/blocks/tone/) beside a [Computer Keyboard](/blocks/computer-keyboard/) makes a playable instrument. Aim a [Particle Emitter](/blocks/particle-emitter/) at it and you have something that can play itself.

## What’s new in BlokDust 2?

Try a [Wavetable](/blocks/wavetable/) oscillator, use a [Prism](/blocks/prism/) to split a beam, or tuck a whole patch inside a [Stage](/blocks/stage/) block.

Turn on Tempo Sync to run emitters, rotating lasers and effects at one patch tempo. In a DAW, they follow the host tempo.

Play in your browser, use the desktop app on macOS or Windows, or add BlokDust to your DAW as VST3 on either desktop platform or AU on macOS. BlokDust also has a standalone iPad app and AUv3.

Save patches to your account, share a link, or publish in the gallery so someone else can open your patch, see how it works and carry on where you left off.

BlokDust 2 has a rebuilt audio and simulation engine for faster, more consistent performance, especially in larger patches. See [What’s New in BlokDust 2](/guide/whats-new/) for the full list of updates.

## Where it started

BlokDust is a collaboration between Luke Twyman, Luke Phillips and Ed Silverton, developed in Brighton, UK, and released in 2016. The rebuild was released in 2026, built by Luke Phillips.

The original project took some influence from the ‘Redstone’ circuits in Minecraft, and its visual style from early Lego and the music controller Reactable.

BlokDust 2 is [open source](https://github.com/lukephills/blokdust-v2). The original release is [on GitHub](https://github.com/BlokDust/BlokDust) too.
